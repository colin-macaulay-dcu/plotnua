From kit to usable space

Make the installation quote explicit.

The maker lists a flatpack kit, installation guide, damp-proof membrane, windows and home delivery, and separately describes installation and customisation services. That does not establish an installed, connected price for your garden.

Ask for a written division between the kit and the work around it: preparation, foundations, assembly, finishing, connections and waste removal. If different people will carry out those jobs, agree who checks that one stage is ready for the next.

The published wall thickness is 44 mm. PlotNua’s reading: that figure alone does not establish the room’s thermal performance. For daily office use, ask about insulation in the floor and roof as well as the walls, glazing, ventilation and heating.

Take these questions to the maker

Price the room you will actually use.

  • Price and tax. What does the starting price buy, is VAT included, and what would the complete agreed specification cost?
  • Size. Which dimensions are external, which are internal, and do decks or other projections sit outside them?
  • Delivery and assembly. Does the delivery offer cover your address and access route? Who unloads, assembles and checks the building?
  • Everyday use. What upgrades, services and finishing would the maker specify for the use you describe?
  • Aftercare. Which warranty applies to the kit and to the installation, and what maintenance is required to keep that cover?

Show the maker a sketch of the room’s furniture and a photograph of its intended position. A useful quote should answer the practical question: what will it take to use this room as you intend, at this address?

Use PlotNua’s garden-room planning guide alongside that discussion. A model name, advertised area or suggested use is not a finding that a building is exempt on your property.
