Garden Room Prices in Ireland

What garden rooms are advertised for, what those prices cover, and how to compare two quotes.

Independent research: 212 priced garden room products from 20 suppliers, in PlotNua’s research snapshot of 7 October 2026. The 177 prices in the published ranges were each last checked against the supplier’s own published information between 28 September 2026 and 7 November 2026. How the evidence is checked

The Short Answer

How much does a garden room cost in Ireland?

In PlotNua’s research, garden room prices come in two very different kinds: a room installed and finished on your site, or a kit that you or a builder put up.

Installed garden room costs

€19,950 – €43,650

Installed and finished: the building, putting it up on your site, the base it stands on and its standard finish. 10 products from 4 suppliers.

Garden room kit prices

€3,624 – €25,891

Kit or self-build: installation is optional or extra, and the base and the electrical connection may be extra too. 167 products from 7 suppliers.

Each range runs from the lowest to the highest price advertised in that group of PlotNua’s research. It is not a typical Irish price, and not the lowest or highest price on the Irish market. Neither is an all-in quotation: delivery, the connection to your house’s electricity and VAT can sit outside an installed price, and a kit price leaves putting the building up to you or a builder you hire.

This guide covers detached garden rooms and garden offices. It does not price extensions or attached sunrooms. Exploring a workspace specifically? See PlotNua’s garden offices in Ireland guide.

Some specialised garden buildings in PlotNua’s research are advertised at substantially higher prices. These are not included in the comparison above because their pricing and installation arrangements differ.

So treat these ranges as a guide to what is advertised, then use the checklist further down to turn any price into one you can actually compare.

Published Prices

Garden room and garden office prices by what the price covers

The same building can be sold three different ways: as a kit you assemble, delivered and built for you, or finished turnkey. Each is a different purchase, so prices are grouped by what the supplier says the price includes, and never mixed. Garden offices sit in the same groups: a garden office is a garden room bought for work, and is priced the same way.

VAT in these prices

Of the 10 installed, finished prices in the range above, 2 say VAT is included, 0 say it is not, and 8 do not say. A price shown “+VAT” or “ex VAT” is less than you will pay, so add VAT before comparing it with a VAT-inclusive price, and ask every supplier for the VAT-inclusive total in writing.

What Affects Price

What affects garden room prices

Two garden rooms of a similar size can be priced very differently. These are the things that most often explain the difference, and the quote checklist further down turns them into questions for each supplier.

  • What the price includesWhether a building is installed and finished on your site, supplied and erected, or sold as a kit is the first thing to establish. This guide gives a separate range for each kind of price it can publish.
  • Size and layoutFloor area, height and the rooms inside change what a price buys. Many prices come without a floor area, and some suppliers measure inside while others measure outside, so ask for the internal floor area.
  • Construction and finishInsulation, glazing, cladding and what is finished inside, such as lining, flooring, sockets, lighting and heating, vary between suppliers and between models.
  • Your garden and accessSloping or soft ground, tight side access and the distance from the house can change the base, the delivery and the electrical connection. See the additional costs to consider →
One Number

Why PlotNua does not publish an average garden room price

An average only means something when every price covers the same thing. These do not. A kit you assemble yourself, a room built and finished on your site, and a price that does not say what it includes would all count equally, so the result would describe the mix of products in the research rather than what you would pay.

So this guide shows the range for each kind of price, how many products and suppliers sit behind it, and how many say VAT is included. The same applies to garden offices: they are priced the same way as garden rooms, and what you pay depends on what is included, not on what the room is called.

Why It Is Hard

Why advertised garden room prices are hard to compare

Before you can tell whether one garden room is dearer than another, you have to know what each price covers. Most of the time, that is not published.

Not every garden-room price covers the same things. Some prices include the building, foundations and installation. Others are for kits or buildings that need additional work. PlotNua separates these so you can compare your options more confidently.

Price per square metre does not solve it. Rooms priced on different bases, or with a different finish inside, can be several times apart per square metre, so a figure like that says more about what was left out than about value.

Outside The Price

Additional garden room costs to consider

These are the costs that most often separate an advertised price from a final quotation. Whether each one applies depends on the supplier and on your garden.

  • VATMany published prices do not say whether VAT is included. On a building of this kind that can be a large part of the bill, so confirm it in writing.
  • Foundations and groundworkA garden room needs a level, load-bearing base. Some suppliers build it, some price it separately, and some leave it to you. Sloping or soft ground, access for machinery and removing soil can all add to the work.
  • DeliverySome prices include delivery nationwide, some only within a region, and some charge by distance. Tight side access can also mean extra handling.
  • InstallationA kit price leaves the building to you or to a builder you hire. A “supplied and erected” price may still stop short of the interior finish.
  • Electrical connectionA room can come wired inside but not connected to your house. The cable run from your consumer unit, and the certificate for that work, are often quoted separately.
  • Building Regulations and planningAbove 25 m² a garden building must meet the Building Regulations in full, and the planning exemption has its own size and position limits. Both can change the specification you pay for. Living in the Garden explains the rules.

Before You Commit

How to compare garden room suppliers: a quote checklist

Ask every supplier the same questions, in writing, before you compare their prices.

  1. Is VAT included in this price?
  2. Is the base or foundation included? If not, what does it need, and who builds it?
  3. Is delivery to my address included, and is my side access suitable?
  4. Is the building put up by your own team, or is this a kit?
  5. What is finished inside: insulation, lining, flooring, sockets, lighting, heating?
  6. Is the electrical connection to the house included, with its certificate?
  7. What is the floor area, measured inside and outside?
  8. Does this design stay within the planning exemption for my garden, and does it need to meet the Building Regulations?
  9. What warranty applies, and to which parts?
  10. Is this a “from” price? If so, what does the version I want cost?

If two suppliers give different answers to the first four questions, their prices were never comparable in the first place.

A Different Question

Garden homes are a separate price question

A garden home, a detached building someone can live in, has a different set of rules since July 2026: its own size range, conditions and notices, and the Building Regulations in full. Very few are advertised with a price yet, so this guide does not put a figure on one.

Living in the Garden explains what changed in 2026 and how the garden room and garden home rules differ.

Questions

Common questions about garden room prices

What is the average cost of a garden room?

A single average would mislead you. Advertised garden room prices cover very different things: a kit you put up yourself, a room built and finished on your site, and a price that does not say what it includes. Averaging them would describe the mix of products in the research, not what you would pay. Compare instead within one kind of price: the published ranges show each kind separately, with how many products and suppliers sit behind it. Why PlotNua does not publish an average explains this in full.

How much does a 3 × 3 m garden room cost?

PlotNua cannot give an honest price for one size. Only 38 of the 212 priced garden rooms in its research publish a floor area at all, and suppliers measure it in different ways, inside or out, so a price for a 3 × 3 m room would rest on too few figures that do not compare. Ask each supplier for the internal floor area and the price for the same finish. The quote checklist covers both.

Is it cheaper to buy a kit than an installed garden room?

Kit prices start lower, but a kit buys less. In PlotNua’s research, prices for a kit or self-build, where installation is optional or extra, ran from €3,624 to €25,891, and installed, finished rooms from €19,950 to €43,650. A kit price leaves putting the building up to you or a builder you hire, and usually leaves out the base and the electrical connection, so price those before comparing. The quote checklist shows what to ask.

Do garden room prices include VAT?

Often the price does not say. Of the 10 installed, finished prices in the range above, 2 say VAT is included, and across all 212 priced garden rooms in PlotNua’s research, 47 do not say either way. Ask for the VAT position in writing.

What else should I budget for besides the room itself?

Foundations and groundwork, delivery, installation, the electrical connection and VAT can all sit outside an advertised price. See what can sit outside the advertised price.

Do I need planning permission for a garden room?

It depends on the room’s size, height, position and use, and on your garden. Living in the Garden explains the 2026 exemption conditions and when the Building Regulations apply.

The Research

How PlotNua checks garden room price evidence

What the research covers

These figures come from PlotNua’s research snapshot of 7 October 2026: 564 garden room products in the Atlas, of which 212 carry a euro price PlotNua can stand behind, from 20 suppliers, with a published route to Irish customers.

How each price is checked

Every price inside a range above was last checked against the supplier’s own published information between 28 September 2026 and 7 November 2026. Ranges are shown only where at least 3 suppliers price on the same basis, and prices on different bases are never combined.

A price stays in a published range only while its last check is no more than 90 days old: PlotNua’s daily check fails as soon as one is older, and the range must then be rechecked. A price PlotNua cannot currently verify is withdrawn rather than estimated, and no figure is added that a supplier does not publish.

Installed or kit

A price counts as installed and finished only where the supplier’s published information covers the building, putting it up on your site, the base it stands on and its standard finish. A kit is counted as a kit. Prices on any other basis, or whose basis is not stated, are counted separately and never mixed into these ranges.

VAT

PlotNua records the VAT position each supplier states, beside the price or in its current terms of sale, and does not check any supplier’s VAT rate or tax treatment. Where a price does not say, PlotNua counts it as not stated rather than assuming either way.

Snapshot date and later rechecks
