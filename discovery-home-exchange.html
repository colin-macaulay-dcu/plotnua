Home Exchange

Your house costs the same whether you are in it or not.

When you go away, your home may be standing empty at exactly the time you are paying for somewhere else to stay.

Why This Discovery?

Who is this for?

Somebody whose house is empty for a week or two most years.

Somebody who has wondered, vaguely, whether that matters — and never had a reason to find out.

This is not about letting your house. Nobody pays anybody. It is about swapping the use of your home for the use of somebody else’s, for the same fortnight or a different one.

An ordinary house, standing available.

The Opportunity

What does this actually mean?

Your house is worth something to somebody else on exactly the days it is worth nothing to you.

That is the whole idea. You stay in their home, they stay in yours, and no money changes hands in either direction. Nobody is renting anything. Nobody becomes a landlord.

What makes your home useful in an exchange is usually not how impressive it is. It is where it is, how many people it sleeps and when it is available.

Those three things are close to the whole list, and the first two came with the house.

It also means you do not need to live somewhere remarkable. A three-bedroom semi twenty minutes from the coast is a straightforward exchange, because what another household needs is a place near whatever they came for and enough beds for everyone.

Why Now

Why does this come up now?

Two things have changed.

The date problem eased. Home exchange used to mean finding one household who wanted your house on precisely the fortnight you wanted theirs. That is a hard match. Points or credits have removed that constraint on some platforms: you host somebody in June, and use what you earn to stay somewhere else in October, with a different household. The two halves no longer have to line up.

And short-term letting is about to be regulated properly. Ireland’s short-term letting register opens on 1 December 2026, and anyone letting accommodation short-term has to be registered by the end of that month. Anyone who hears about that could reasonably assume it closes the door on any arrangement involving their house and a stranger.

It is worth understanding why a straight swap is a different arrangement — and worth knowing where that is clear and where it is not.

Today

What can you do today?

Three ways, and the one that suits you depends on your dates more than your house.

If you can both travel at the same time — a straight swap

The simplest version. You agree a fortnight with another household, you stay in their home, they stay in yours, and everybody goes home afterwards.

No money moves. The arrangement is between the two households, and a platform handles the introductions, the profiles and the record of what was agreed.

The catch is the matching. Both households have to want the same two weeks.

If your dates do not line up — points or credits

Some platforms let you host without going anywhere yourself. A night at your home is worth a set number of points; hosting for a few nights earns you a few nights’ worth. You spend them later, somewhere else, with somebody who has never seen your house.

One honest warning. Points are not money, but they have value. Ireland’s new letting rules are written around accommodation provided for payment, and whether points count as payment has not been decided or written down anywhere we could find. A straight swap sits more clearly outside those rules than a points arrangement does. See Looking Ahead, below.

If you have a second property — exchanging the one you are not in

If you own a holiday home or an inherited house, it can be exchanged without anyone entering your own home at all.

It removes the question of somebody staying in your own home. And it makes a house that sits empty for much of the year genuinely useful.

The same house, a fortnight later, with somebody else in it.

Day to Day

What does it change day to day?

Nothing at all, until the fortnight arrives.

Then it is mostly preparation: emptying a shelf or two, putting away what you would rather not have handled, leaving instructions for the heating and the bins, and arranging how the keys get from you to them.

You may never meet them. Keys can pass through a neighbour, a lockbox or a key safe, and the two households can travel in opposite directions on the same day.

What changes is smaller than it sounds and stranger than it sounds. Somebody is living in your house — cooking in your kitchen, sleeping in your bed — while you are doing exactly the same in theirs.

Before You Start

What should you think about before you start?

Start with the one that is not about the house.

Would you be comfortable with another family living in your home for a fortnight? Some people are, immediately. Some are not, and never will be. If that is you, this Discovery is not for your house, and that is a perfectly good answer to reach early.

Then the practical ones. How many weeks a year is the house genuinely empty? Are those weeks fixed by school terms? How many people can it sleep — really, not at a push? Is there parking, and does that matter where you are?

Then the four that need proper thought.

Insurance, and it runs the opposite way to expectation. Irish home policies commonly restrict cover once a house has stood empty for a prolonged period, with the exact period depending on the policy. An empty house is the insurance question. But another household living in your home during an exchange raises a separate question again, and whether that counts as the house being occupied is not something we could establish for the Irish market. Ask your own insurer, in writing, before you agree dates. Any protection a platform offers sits on top of your policy — it does not replace it.

The short-term letting register. It opens on 1 December 2026. The rules define a host as somebody providing accommodation for payment, and the register applies to accommodation offered for rent. In a straight swap nobody pays anybody. On the wording of the rules, a no-payment exchange is a different arrangement from a short-term let — but nobody has published a ruling on home exchange specifically, so this is a reading of the rules rather than a settled position. If money ever changes hands, it stops being an exchange and all the ordinary rules apply.

Tax. No Revenue guidance addressing reciprocal home exchange was found. That is not the same as being told there is nothing to consider, and it should not be treated as it. Take your own advice rather than relying on the absence of a rule.

Your mortgage, and your lease if you have one. Mortgage conditions and apartment or management-company rules can both restrict who may occupy a property. We have not verified what any particular lender or management company allows. Both are worth checking before you list.

PlotNua does not arrange exchanges, does not take a share of anything, and cannot tell you whether your house will be wanted.

Looking Ahead

Looking Ahead

Everything above can be done in Ireland today. What follows is less settled.

The points question

The rules coming into force in December are built around accommodation provided for payment. Points are not money, but they do have value — you earn them by hosting and spend them on somewhere to stay.

Whether that counts as payment has not been decided, and nothing published answers it. A straight swap sits more clearly outside these rules than a points arrangement does. If you are exchanging on points, that is worth asking about rather than assuming.

What would settle it

Guidance from Fáilte Ireland or the Department naming home exchange, one way or the other.

Until then this stays a question rather than a rule, and we would rather tell you that than tidy it away.

Why the house keeps its value either way

Whatever happens to the rules, the thing that makes a house worth exchanging does not move. It is where the house is, how many it sleeps and when it is free — and none of those depends on a register.

Frequently Asked Questions

Questions this Discovery tends to raise.

Is this the same as renting out my house?
No. In an exchange nobody pays anybody. You are not a landlord, no rent passes between the households, and the arrangement is that each of you gets somewhere to stay. That is a different thing from a short-term let, where a guest pays to stay.

Do I get paid?
No, and it is worth being clear about it. No money reaches you. You pay an annual membership to the platform, and what you get back is somewhere to stay that you did not have to pay for. The benefit is real, but it is accommodation you avoid paying for rather than income.

Do we have to travel on the same dates?
Not necessarily. Some platforms run on points: you host somebody at a time that suits you, and spend what you earn later, somewhere else, with a different household. On those platforms your dates and theirs do not have to match.

What about insurance?
This is the one to sort first. Irish policies commonly restrict cover once a house has been empty for a prolonged period — how long depends on your policy. Another household staying in your home raises a separate question again, and we could not establish how Irish insurers treat it. Ask yours in writing before you agree anything. Platform protection sits on top of your own cover, not instead of it.

What if something gets damaged?
A platform may hold a deposit and offer cover for accidental damage, cancellation and homes that turn out not to match their listing. Read what yours actually covers, and remember it is separate from your household insurance.

Does the new short-term letting register apply to me?
The register opens on 1 December 2026 and applies to hosts offering accommodation for payment, for periods up to 21 nights. In a straight swap nobody pays anybody, so on the wording of the rules there is no host and nothing to register. Nobody has published a ruling on home exchange specifically, so treat that as a reading of the rules rather than settled. Points are a separate question — see Looking Ahead.

Does my house need to be somewhere remarkable?
No. What another household needs is a place near whatever they came for and enough beds. People exchange to visit family, to walk, to work somewhere for a month.

What if I live in an apartment, or still have a mortgage?
Apartments can be exchanged. But management-company rules and mortgage conditions can both restrict who is allowed to occupy a property, and we have not verified what any particular lender or management company permits. Check both before you list.
