Home Exchange

Your house costs the same whether you are in it or not.

When you go away, your home may be standing empty at exactly the time you are paying for somewhere else to stay.

Could this work for your property?

PlotNua is designing a property check for this Discovery.

This property check is being prepared

Until it is ready, keep reading to see what matters.

The Opportunity

Could your house be exchanged?

Nobody pays anybody. You stay in their home, they stay in yours — or, on some platforms, at a different time entirely.

  • Empty weeks A week or two most years when the house is genuinely empty. How many, and whether they are fixed by school terms, decides more than the house does.
  • Beds How many people it can sleep — really, not at a push. Parking matters too, depending on where you are.
  • Comfort Whether you would be comfortable with another family living in your home for a fortnight. Some people are, immediately. Some are not, and never will be. That is a perfectly good answer to reach early.
What your home unlocks

Your house is worth something to somebody else on exactly the days it is worth nothing to you.

Nobody is renting anything, and nobody becomes a landlord.

  • Somewhere to stay that you did not have to pay for.No money reaches you. You pay an annual membership to the platform, and what you get back is accommodation you avoid paying for rather than income.
What makes a home exchangeable
  • Where it is. Near whatever the other household came for. It does not have to be somewhere remarkable. People exchange to visit family, to walk, to work somewhere for a month.
  • How many it sleeps. Enough beds for everyone is most of the requirement.
  • When it is free. The weeks the house is standing empty anyway. Close to the whole list — and the first two came with the house.
How it could work
  • A straight swap You agree a fortnight with another household. You stay in their home, they stay in yours, and everybody goes home afterwards. The catch is the matching: both households have to want the same two weeks. A platform handles the introductions, the profiles and the record of what was agreed.
  • Points or credits A night at your home is worth a set number of points. Host now, and spend what you earn later, somewhere else. Your dates and theirs never have to line up. Whether points count as payment under the new rules is a separate question — see below.
  • A second property A holiday home or an inherited house can be exchanged without anyone entering your own home at all. And it makes a house that sits empty for much of the year genuinely useful.
  • Insurance Irish policies commonly restrict cover once a house has stood empty for a prolonged period. Whether an exchange counts as the house being occupied is not something we could establish for the Irish market. Ask your own insurer, in writing, before you agree dates. Any protection a platform offers sits on top of your policy, not instead of it.
  • The register Ireland’s short-term letting register opens on 1 December 2026 and applies to accommodation offered for payment. In a straight swap nobody pays anybody, so on the wording of the rules there is no host and nothing to register. Nobody has published a ruling on home exchange specifically, so treat that as a reading of the rules rather than a settled position.
  • What is not settled Whether points count as payment has not been decided, and no Revenue guidance addressing reciprocal home exchange was found. That is not the same as being told there is nothing to consider, and it should not be treated as it. Guidance from Fáilte Ireland or the Department naming home exchange would settle it.
  • Mortgage and lease Mortgage conditions and management-company rules can both restrict who may occupy a property. We have not verified what any particular lender or management company allows. Both are worth checking before you list.

Why this is not letting your house
In an exchange nobody pays anybody. You are not a landlord, no rent passes between the households, and the arrangement is that each of you gets somewhere to stay. That is a different thing from a short-term let, where a guest pays to stay.

The register. It opens on 1 December 2026, and anyone letting accommodation short-term has to be registered by the end of that month. The rules define a host as somebody providing accommodation for payment, for periods up to 21 nights. On the wording of the rules a no-payment exchange is a different arrangement from a short-term let — but nobody has published a ruling on home exchange specifically, so this is a reading of the rules rather than a settled position. If money ever changes hands, it stops being an exchange and all the ordinary rules apply.

Points. The rules are built around accommodation provided for payment. Points are not money, but they do have value: you earn them by hosting and spend them on somewhere to stay. Whether that counts as payment has not been decided, and nothing published answers it. A straight swap sits more clearly outside these rules than a points arrangement does. If you are exchanging on points, that is worth asking about rather than assuming.

Tax. No Revenue guidance addressing reciprocal home exchange was found. That is not the same as being told there is nothing to consider. Take your own advice rather than relying on the absence of a rule.

What the fortnight actually involves
Nothing at all changes until the fortnight arrives. Then it is mostly preparation: emptying a shelf or two, putting away what you would rather not have handled, leaving instructions for the heating and the bins, and arranging how the keys get from you to them.

You may never meet them. Keys can pass through a neighbour, a lockbox or a key safe, and the two households can travel in opposite directions on the same day.

What changes is smaller than it sounds and stranger than it sounds. Somebody is living in your house — cooking in your kitchen, sleeping in your bed — while you are doing exactly the same in theirs.

What a platform does, and what it does not
A platform handles the introductions, the profiles and the record of what was agreed, and you pay it an annual membership. It may hold a deposit and offer cover for accidental damage, cancellation, and homes that turn out not to match their listing. Read what yours actually covers, and remember it is separate from your household insurance.

Apartments can be exchanged, subject to the management-company and mortgage conditions above.

PlotNua does not arrange exchanges, does not take a share of anything, and cannot tell you whether your house will be wanted.

The same house, a fortnight later, with somebody else in it.

The value may not be what your home earns. It may be where it lets you go.
