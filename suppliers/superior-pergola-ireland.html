Before you ask for a price

How a quote works.

Everything is made to measure, so the published price is where a quote starts, not what you will pay.

  1. The starting price

    Each range has a published from-price that includes fitting. Your price depends on size, roof, sides and extras.

  2. A visit to measure

    The supplier gives the final price once it has seen and measured the space.

  3. What to have ready

    Rough width and depth, whether it fixes to the house wall or stands free, and photos of the spot.

  4. What to ask

    Which roof suits your light and rain; how water drains off it; whether sides or doors can be added later; what the warranty covers for your build; and how long from order to fitting.

From-prices were checked on superiorpergola.ie on 4 November 2026 and can change. The supplier describes its structures as weather-resistant rather than fully waterproof, and its terms make the customer responsible for planning and building-regulation compliance.

For Irish homeowners

Worth checking first.

General points, not an assessment of your property.

Planning

Many open pergolas in a back garden are exempted development, but limits apply. A front porch is exempt only if it is no more than 2 m² in floor area and at least 2 m from a public road, within height limits. A glazed room attached to the house can count as an extension. Check with your local authority before you order.

Glass or polycarbonate

Glass is clearer and quieter in rain; polycarbonate is lighter and usually cheaper. Tinted glass cuts glare and summer heat but also winter light.

Open or closed in

An open pergola is shelter. Adding glass sides and doors makes it a room, which changes how you'll use it, how warm it gets and possibly the planning position.

Fixing and drainage

Ask how the frame fixes to the house or the ground, and where the rainwater goes.
