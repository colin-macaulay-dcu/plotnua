Before you budget

How The Pod Factory prices a pod.

There is no published price to compare. A quote for your site is the starting point; ask what it includes.

Price

No price list is published. Every pod is bespoke, so the supplier prices each one for the client; ask for a quote.

Delivery and installation

Pods are delivered by lorry and craned into place, anywhere with access for the trucks and crane.

Groundworks

The site's groundworks must be completed before the pod arrives. The site does not say whether these are in the quote; ask.

Services

Water and electricity are connected to the services already on site, as part of the installation.

Heating

ATC radiators are fitted as standard. Solar panels, heat pumps and triple glazing can be added.

VAT

Not stated.

Lead time: the supplier's pages give about 10 weeks to delivery (modular homes page) and as little as twelve weeks (FAQ) once the design is finalised. Its showroom near Dunshaughlin, Co. Meath, can be visited by appointment, including out of hours.

For Irish homeowners

Worth checking first.

General points, not an assessment of your property.

Planning

Whether a pod needs planning permission depends on its size, its use and your site. The Pod Factory advises asking your local planning department; it also publishes its own guidance on the newer exemptions. A pod you live in is treated differently from a garden studio.

Crane access

Pods arrive finished and are lifted in by crane, so there has to be a route for the lorry and the crane.

Groundworks first

The base and groundworks are done before delivery. Agree who does them and what they cost before you sign.

The frame

The pods are framed in light gauge steel made by the supplier's sister company, Metal Frame Construction, which the supplier says holds NSAI and UKAS accreditations. Ask for the certificates that apply to your build.
