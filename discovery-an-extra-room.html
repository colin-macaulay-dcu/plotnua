Create an Extra Room

Without extending the house.

Most Irish gardens have space for one.

Why This Discovery?

Where does an extra room go?

There are three ways to get another room. Extend the house, convert the attic or the garage, or build one in the garden.

This page is about adding a room in the garden. If you are still deciding how to create the space — or need somewhere for somebody to live — the two options below will take you to the right Discovery.

Using It

How can you use it?

Most of what people want a garden room for is already allowed.

  • Everyday use. Home office, art studio, music room, gym, hobby room, playroom, teenage room, study, reading room, wellness room.
  • Ask first. Teaching, seeing clients or running a business from it. It depends on how big it gets, so ring the council before you build.
  • Sleeping. That is covered by Build a Home in Your Garden.
The Rules

What are the rules?

Since 27 July 2026 you can build up to 30 m² in your back garden with no planning application at all. 30 m² is about 5 m × 6 m. The limit before that was 25 m².

  • Up to 30 m². Sheds, garages and greenhouses already in the garden count towards it, so measure what is out there first. A 12 m² shed leaves you 18 m². If somebody might live in the garden one day, size this building carefully — a home in the garden has its own allowance, and what you build now counts against it.
  • Up to 4 m high with a tiled or slated pitched roof. 3 m for any other roof.
  • Behind the front of the house. Back garden or side.
  • Keep 25 m² of garden. That much open space stays at the back or side.
  • Match the house if it goes to the side. Behind the house it does not matter.

These are the conditions in the 2026 exempted development regulations. Whether your own garden meets all of them depends on its size, shape and what is already built on it.

Other Requirements

What else applies?

Planning is one part. These are the others, and what each one means for a garden room.

Building Regulations

Usually relevant. They are separate from planning, and being exempt from one does not exempt you from the other.

They set how the building is put together: structure, insulation, ventilation and electrical work. Which parts apply depends on the size, the construction and the use.

What to do: nothing yet. PlotNua gathers what each supplier states about how their buildings are built, and shows it beside the price. Building Regulations — gov.ie

Building Control

Depends on the project. This is the local authority side: notices, inspection and certification.

Whether a commencement notice is needed is not the same for every garden room. The answer depends on the room’s size, construction and intended use.

What to do: one call to your local authority's Building Control section settles it before you order. Building Control — gov.ie

Fire safety

Worth checking on every build. Three things affect it: how close the room sits to the house and the boundary, what it is made of, and how you get out of it.

Use matters too. A room with a stove or a kiln is not an empty study.

What to do: agree the position and the wall build-up before the base goes in. Building standards and technical guidance — gov.ie

Protected structures

Only if your house is on the list. Every local authority keeps a Record of Protected Structures, and where a house is on it, exempted development rights can be restricted in the grounds as well as the house.

What to do: check the Record for your area, and ask the conservation officer if you are on it. Protected structures — Citizens Information

Architectural conservation areas

Only if your address is inside one. These are designated in the county or city development plan, and extra controls can apply to how a structure looks and where it sits.

A room behind the house is less likely to be affected than one visible from the road. That is the council's judgement rather than ours.

What to do: the development plan lists them, and the planning office will confirm your address in one call.

Cost

What does it cost?

Three broad levels, taken from garden rooms the PlotNua currently holds a published price for.

From about €3,600

Basic or seasonal

A timber garden building, lightly insulated or not insulated. Comfortable in spring and autumn, cold in January.

For example: Gardenhouse24 Monaco 28 at €3,624, MCD Garden Sheds Apex Cabin at €5,469.

About €8,000 to €18,000

Insulated, year-round

The largest group by far. Insulated walls, floor and roof, double glazing and a finished interior you can work in all year.

For example: Sprout Pod 6m at €10,250, Adman Multiroom 3m × 5.2m at €12,450, SIPS Garden Room POPPY at €12,750.

€18,000 and above

Larger or higher specification

More floor area, more glazing, better heating, higher internal finish. The dearest garden room in the Atlas is €67,950.

For example: Iglucraft Igluoffice 2 at €48,000, Concept Modular Cabin 25m² at €49,750.

Usually extra: the base, delivery, installation, electricity, heating and any groundworks. PlotNua records what each published price covers, so two prices are compared on the same terms.

Two things the Atlas is still gathering. Suppliers do not consistently publish whether VAT is included, or the floor area. So these are price bands with real examples rather than a price per square metre.

PlotNua is collecting both, and the bands will sharpen as it does. Indicative prices reviewed in August 2026.

Your Property

Would this work on your property?

  • Getting to it. Side access makes the build simpler. If the only way in is through the house, that changes how it is built.
  • Power and heating. Easier to plan at the start than to add later.

Frequently Asked Questions

Common questions.

How long does it take?
A single room is one of the quicker home projects — often a matter of weeks, depending on the design and how easy the garden is to get into.

Can someone sleep in it?
Not under these rules. A separate rule covers a one-bedroom home in the garden, with its own size and conditions — Build a Home in Your Garden.

Can I work from it?
Your own work, yes. Teaching, clients or a business may need permission depending on how much of it there is. Ring the council first.

Will it add value?
That depends on the house and the area, and we would rather say so than make up a figure. A well-insulated room that suits more than one use holds its value best.
