Coast, light and layers.

Coastal art need not be a literal seascape. Layered paint, printed texture and drawn line can hold the feel of a shoreline: weather, tide lines, rock and light moving on water, more than a view of one particular beach. Small coastal works on paper reward a close look, and several from one series read like a walk along the same stretch of coast.

Choosing a coastal work.

  • Hang a series together. Works from one series sit well side by side, in a row or a grid. Ask the artist about choosing more than one.
  • Ask about framing. Many coastal works are made on heavy paper or board. Ask whether a work is framed and glazed, and whether the size given includes the frame.
  • Keep it from the sun. Works on paper are sensitive to light: hang them away from direct sun and damp, in a hallway, a bedroom or a study.
  • Measure the wall. Small works can look lost on a wide wall; the art size calculator helps you judge a group.
