What makes a print original.

An original print starts on a screen, a plate or a block that the artist prepares. Each colour or layer is printed by hand, so every sheet is made separately and can differ slightly from the next. A reproduction, by contrast, copies an existing artwork by photographic or digital means.

Screen prints build an image in flat, saturated layers of colour, one stencil at a time. Intaglio prints are etched into a metal plate and inked into its grooves, which gives a fine, slightly embossed line. A monoprint is printed once and cannot be repeated: like a painting, it is one of a kind.

Editions and numbers.

An edition is the number of impressions the artist chooses to print. A pencilled number such as 3/10 means the third of ten. A smaller edition makes each impression scarcer, but size alone does not make a print better: look at the image, the paper and the printing.

Framing and care.

  • Frame behind glazing. Use an acid-free mount and keep the print away from direct sun and damp.
  • Handle by the edges. Unframed sheets should be held by the margins, with clean, dry hands.
  • Ask about signing. Ask the artist whether the print is signed and numbered, and keep any note or certificate with it.
