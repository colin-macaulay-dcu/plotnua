Choosing for the space.

A workplace is seen differently from a home: by more people, often in passing, and under office lighting. A few things to weigh before you shortlist a work.

Reception and entrance areas

Work here is usually seen from across the room and on the way past, so scale matters. Works of 100 cm or more tend to hold a wall like this. Compare a work’s size with your wall in the art size calculator.

Meeting rooms and boardrooms

Here a work is seen from a seat, close up and for an hour at a time. A medium-sized piece hung at seated eye level can suit a room better than the largest canvas. Browse all available artworks.

Hotels, restaurants and cafés

Think about daylight, heat, steam and how close guests will be. Ask the artist whether the work is framed or glazed and how it should be cared for in a busy room.

Interior designers and architects

Every work page gives the artist’s medium, materials and dimensions. Whether a work can be held while a scheme is approved is the artist’s decision: ask them directly.

What to ask the artist.

Every enquiry goes to the artist, who replies to you directly. Agree these points with them in writing before you pay.

  • The total price. What the asking price includes: framing, VAT where it applies, and delivery.
  • An invoice. Whether the artist can invoice your business by name.
  • Weight and hanging. Whether the work is framed, what it weighs, and what fixings it needs. Check that the wall can take it.
  • Delivery and access. Collection or delivery, who hangs the work, and whether it fits through doors, stairs and lifts on the way in.
  • Care. How to look after the work in your space: light, heat, humidity and cleaning.
  • Timing. When the work can be collected or delivered.

To ask about a particular work, open it and use Enquire about this work. To ask an artist about their wider practice, use the enquiry on their gallery page. If you are considering works by more than one artist, contact each artist separately: each handles their own sales.
