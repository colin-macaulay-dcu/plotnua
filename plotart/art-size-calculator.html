Room to make your own decision

What size art should go above a sofa?

One useful starting point is a work or group spanning about two-thirds to three-quarters of the furniture width. A 200 cm sofa therefore suggests roughly 133–150 cm across. Smaller and larger choices can work too: the surrounding space, the work itself and the effect you want matter.

This proportion is a common guideline, also described in Saatchi Art’s living-room art guide. It is a styling suggestion, not a fitting or installation specification.

For a clear wall

Measure the usable space between corners, doors, windows, shelves and other obstructions. In clear-wall mode, the tool compares the entered artwork width with that usable width. It does not prescribe a particular proportion.

For several artworks together

Use the total outer width and height of the arrangement, including gaps between the frames. The outline then represents the whole group rather than a single work.

Check the frame and the real wall

Artists may list the artwork size without a frame. Confirm the outer dimensions before buying. Mark the outline with removable paper to judge the scale in your actual space. Ask about suitable fixings and professional installation where needed.

Find a work you want to live with.

Once you have a sense of scale, explore the artists and their work. Read the medium, dimensions and availability on each artwork page, and ask the artist about framing and viewing.
