How a commission works

  1. Choose an artist

    Look through the galleries of the artists listed below and find work that speaks to you. You can also see available original works by every PlotArt artist.

  2. Send an enquiry through PlotArt

    On the artist’s gallery, use “Enquire about a commission”, or “Enquire through PlotArt”. PlotArt passes your name, email address and message to the artist, who replies to you directly.

  3. Talk through the brief

    Tell the artist what you have in mind: the room or wall, the size, the colours or mood, your budget and any date that matters. Point to the works in their gallery that drew you to them; most artists find that more useful than a detailed instruction.

  4. Agree it with the artist

    The artist decides whether to take the commission on. The size, subject, price, timescale, payment and delivery are agreed between you and the artist. PlotArt doesn’t take payment.

What to agree before work begins

Every artist works differently, and PlotArt doesn’t set commission terms. These are the questions people typically settle with the artist, ideally in writing, before any work starts.

  • The brief. What the work is about, and how much freedom the artist has. Will you see sketches, colour studies or a photograph of the work in progress, and at what stage?
  • Size and medium. The finished dimensions in centimetres, whether they include a frame or mount, and the medium: a painting, a work on paper, a print or mixed media.
  • Budget. The total price, and what it covers: framing, any VAT that applies, packing, collection or delivery, and hanging.
  • Timeline. When the artist can begin, roughly how long the work will take, and whether there is a date you need it by. Artists’ schedules vary, so ask early if timing matters.
  • Deposit and payment. Whether the artist asks for a deposit, when the balance is due, and what happens to the deposit if either of you decides not to go ahead.
  • Changes. How much feedback is part of the process, and what happens if you would like changes once the work is finished.
  • Images and rights. Copyright usually stays with the artist unless you agree otherwise. Ask whether the artist would like to photograph the finished work, and how you may share images of it.

Choosing the size

Size is often the reason to commission: a wall, alcove or stairwell where nothing already available is quite right. Measure the space, note the distance from which the work will usually be seen, and look at the light at different times of day.

The free art size calculator suggests a size in centimetres for a wall or a piece of furniture, which gives you and the artist a shared starting point. For a large space, look at the large artworks of 100 cm or more already available: an artist’s existing large work shows how they handle scale.

Large works can take longer to make and to dry, and are harder to move. Talk to the artist early about access, hanging and how the finished work will reach you.

Price guidance

Commission prices are set by each artist and depend on size, medium, complexity and time. PlotArt doesn’t publish commission prices.

As a guide, the list above shows each artist’s current asking prices: the lowest and highest price of their works available on PlotArt right now, taken from their published gallery and updated whenever they publish or sell work. It is an indication of how the artist prices finished work, not a quote. A commission may cost more or less, so ask the artist for a price based on your brief.

For how artwork prices work more generally, and what to ask before you pay, read Buying original art in Ireland. If you would rather choose a finished piece, see choosing art for your living room or browse all available artworks.

Artists stay in control of their commissions: each artist chooses whether to be listed here, whether to accept a commission, and their own prices, timescales and arrangements. An enquiry isn’t a booking, and nothing is agreed until you and the artist agree it.
