An Irish homeowner layer for residential compute

PlotNua is an Irish property-decision platform. We have built a homeowner readiness layer for this category already, before approaching anyone, because it is the part we can build without you and the part that takes longest. This note sets out what exists, what it cannot yet do, and what we would need from SPAN to make it useful.

Prepared 24 September 2026 Private link No prior contact
What PlotNua is

PlotNua does the homework. The homeowner makes the decision.

Most Irish homeowners do not know what their property could become. PlotNua helps them discover a possibility, then understand the evidence they would need to evaluate it — before they talk to anyone selling anything.

Each possibility is a Discovery: an editorial article that explains what is genuinely happening, followed by a Property Check that reads the homeowner's own answers against evidence PlotNua holds and tells them where their property actually stands.

The discipline that makes it work is the one we apply hardest to ourselves: where PlotNua cannot stand over something, it says so rather than filling the gap. That is why this note contains as much about what we do not know as about what we have built.

PlotNua already exists

Behind the homeowner experience is a structured evidence platform.

Atlas is PlotNua's property-intelligence base. It is where every homeowner-facing finding comes from, and every fact in it carries the source it came from and the date it was read. Counted directly from the base on 24 September 2026:

  • 930 Products across 193 organisations
  • 8,199 Structured product facts recorded against 82 defined features
  • 722 Pricing records 552 of them verified
  • 963 Evidence sources plus 379 availability records

These are records in a product-intelligence database. They are not users, customers, visitors, leads, sign-ups or demand of any kind. PlotNua is not publishing a homeowner audience figure here because it is not claiming one.

On the public site today there are 10 Discoveries and 8 Property Checks — live decision journeys, not prototypes. The readiness work below is the ninth.

Why XFRA caught our attention

A compute architecture that begins at the house.

From SPAN's own published material: XFRA places compute nodes in residential and small-commercial locations; a SPAN Panel and a battery are required; the nodes use liquid-cooled NVIDIA RTX PRO 6000 Blackwell Server Edition GPUs in a closed-loop system targeting roughly 60 dB; the first proof of concept is 100 US new-construction homes, with broader expansion planned from 2027.

From the Sunrun investor release: a behind-the-meter residential compute arrangement beginning in new-construction communities in Texas and other named markets, in which the host receives a smart panel, multi-battery storage and solar on a pre-paid lease at no cost, whole-home backup, and ongoing compensation for hosting — with workloads scheduled by latency requirement and real-time energy availability at each home.

SPAN also states plainly that XFRA is not currently available outside the US, that there is no waitlist, and that it is not yet sharing technical detail beyond its public resources. We have taken all three at face value, and this note is built on that basis.

What we built

A provider-neutral residential compute readiness check, live in the product.

It is a working Property Check on plotnua.ie, built on the same shell as the eight before it. Eleven questions across five evidence groups — the property, the electricity supply, the energy already there, the connectivity, and the physical space and access.

It names no provider anywhere a homeowner can read. If a different company reached Ireland first, not one sentence would need rewriting. That is deliberate: a readiness layer that is really a funnel for one company is worth nothing to the homeowner and not much to the company.

There is no score, and that is the point

PlotNua does not tell a homeowner their property is a given percentage suitable, because a percentage claims we know what 100% would be. Every technical requirement is unpublished, so there is no denominator. A number invented to fill that gap would be false precision, and it would be the first thing an engineer disproved.

So the engine returns one of three evidence-controlled states, and it is built so that nothing a homeowner tells us can ever become verified technical evidence — every stored record carries provider technical validation: not assessed, in a field whose vocabulary has no other value in it.

What the homeowner actually sees

Findings are grouped and separated by kind. A tick is something the property has. A question mark is something still to establish — their gap, which an answer closes. A dash is something they have told us is not there, which is knowledge, not absence of it.

Underneath every result, unconditionally, sit the two facts most likely to be misunderstood: that this is not commercially available through PlotNua in Ireland, and that nobody has published what a property would have to meet.

  1. DiscoveryAn editorial article explains what is genuinely happening in this category, internationally, with sources.
  2. Homeowner educationEleven plain questions. “I don't know” is a first-class answer everywhere it appears, and the connection capacity question never requires anyone to find their MIC.
  3. Preliminary property screeningThree evidence-controlled states. Nothing is ruled out on tenure, and the closed state needs two positive statements from the homeowner about their own property.
  4. Evidence gapsEvery unknown is named and returned to the homeowner as something they could go and find out.
  5. Interest captureAn optional register. Opt-in, separable consent, Eircode routing key only — never the full code.
  6. Geographic aggregationCounts by county and routing key, with any cell below five suppressed.
  7. Provider technical qualificationNot built, and not buildable, until the requirements below exist.
What the data could become

An aggregate picture of the Irish side, if one ever forms.

The register and the aggregate instrument are built and tested. Given a population, they would answer how many properties are registered, how many report fibre to the home, how many have solar or a battery installed, how many report a known connection capacity or three-phase supply, how they cluster by county and routing key, how many are new build against existing stock, and which evidence gaps recur.

The register is not open and holds no entries. There is no interest population, no demand figure and no waiting list, and PlotNua will not present one until real entries exist. Any count shown to anyone would be derived from the register against a clearly defined state, or it would not be shown.

Ireland

Why Ireland is at least worth a look.

Compact, English-speaking, EU, and unusually well connected for its size. At Q2 2026 ComReg reports fibre to the premises available at 87% of Irish premises, with every county at or above 80%, and gigabit broadband at 92%. There are 1.13 million FTTP subscriber lines, 63% of all fixed broadband lines.

On the electrical side, ESB Networks offers a standard domestic connection of 12 kVA, with 16 kVA, 20 kVA and 29 kVA available, 29 kVA being the upper limit of domestic MIC. Smart meters and half-hourly tariffs are in the market, and ESB Networks is developing flexibility services.

National infrastructure statistics describe a country, not a house. None of the above says anything about whether any individual Irish property could host anything, and PlotNua does not present it as if it did.

What we would need

The criteria that would turn screening into qualification.

PlotNua's check is preliminary by construction, and it will stay preliminary until there is something to screen against. These are not demands and there is no expectation of an answer today — they are simply the fields that are currently empty, and the reason the readiness layer stops where it stops.

Published, and already used
  • Compute nodes in residential and small-commercial locations
  • SPAN Panel and battery required
  • Liquid-cooled NVIDIA RTX PRO 6000 Blackwell Server Edition GPUs
  • Closed-loop cooling, roughly 60 dB target
  • 100-home US new-construction proof of concept
  • Broader expansion planned from 2027
  • Not currently available outside the US · no waitlist
  • Host compensation exists; no amount published
Not published, and needed to qualify a property
  • Continuous electrical load, in kW
  • Maximum electrical load, in kW
  • 230V / 50Hz compatibility
  • Minimum connection capacity, against Ireland's 12–29 kVA domestic range
  • Whether three phase is required
  • Minimum bandwidth, and minimum upload
  • Maximum tolerable latency
  • Physical dimensions and siting clearances
  • European certification and conformity route
  • Deployment model outside new-build communities, if any
  • Any European timetable

Give PlotNua the left-hand column in numbers and the right-hand column becomes a filter. Until then, a homeowner being told their property is suitable would be being told something nobody knows.

The ask

Could an Irish homeowner readiness layer be useful to you?

That is the whole question. Not whether SPAN has any Irish plans, and not whether we could work together — simply whether the homeowner and property side of this, built to the standard above, would be worth anything to XFRA as it develops and future markets are considered.

If it would, the right person is probably whoever holds XFRA market development or partnerships. If it would not, that is a genuinely useful answer too, and PlotNua will carry on building the Irish layer either way.
