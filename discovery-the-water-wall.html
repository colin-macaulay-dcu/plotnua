The Water Wall

What if your garden wall could store the rain?

A slim storage wall can collect the rain from your roof and keep it for the garden, standing quietly along a boundary you already have.

AI-generated illustration — not a real installation.

01

The possibility

What if your garden wall could store the rain?

Rain falls on the roof. Most of it goes down the drain.

Every downpipe carries the rain from your roof straight to a drain. A few weeks later, in a dry spell, the same garden is watered from the tap.

A rainwater-storage wall keeps some of that water. It is a tall, slim tank, about the depth of a garden bench, that stands along a boundary or the side of a house and fills from the downpipe. Its face can be planted, screened or simply left as a clean, dark plane.

The idea

More than a water butt.

A water butt holds enough for a few watering cans. A storage wall is a different scale of idea: about a thousand litres in a single slim panel, and a boundary that does a job.

03

How it works

From the roof to the flower bed.

The principle is the same as any rainwater tank. What changes is the shape: the storage becomes part of the garden’s architecture.

How a rainwater-storage wall works

  1. 1RoofRain falls on the roof. As a rule of thumb, 1 mm of rain on 1 m² of roof gives about 1 litre of water, a little less once some is lost to splash and evaporation.
  2. 2Gutter and downpipeThe gutter collects it and the downpipe carries it down, as it does today.
  3. 3Downpipe filterA filter or diverter on the downpipe keeps leaves and debris out and sends clean water to the storage.
  4. 4Storage wallThe water collects in the wall, which stands on its own prepared footing. A closed top keeps out light, children and leaves.
  5. 5OverflowWhen the wall is full, the extra water carries on down the downpipe to the drain, exactly as it does now.
  6. 6Garden irrigationA tap at the base fills a watering can or a hose. A small pump can feed a drip line to beds and pots.
Using the water

For the garden, not the kitchen

Stored rainwater suits watering beds, pots, vegetables and young trees, and washing tools or a bike. It isn’t treated, so it isn’t for drinking or cooking, and a garden-only system has no connection to the mains supply.

A tap and a watering can need nothing more. A pump that feeds a drip line or a timer needs power: where that means new outdoor wiring, use a Registered Electrical Contractor.

04

The practicalities

Is it right for your garden?

A full storage wall is heavy, tall and close to a boundary. That’s why a full-sized one is not a simple DIY project. These are the questions to settle first.

Before anything is ordered

What a storage wall asks of a garden.

What each point depends on is your site: the ground, the wall beside it, the drains and your neighbours.

Established

Physical facts and the rules that apply. Each item notes who should confirm it for your garden.

Weight when filled

About a tonne for every thousand litres

A litre of water weighs a kilogram, so a full 1,000-litre module weighs about a tonne, plus the tank itself, on a narrow strip of ground. That load has to be carried by a properly prepared footing, not by soil, paving or an old patio.

Structural support

A footing designed for the load

The base should be designed for the filled weight and your ground, by the installer or a structural engineer. Makers publish base requirements for their own tanks: follow them, and keep the paperwork.

The wall behind

An existing garden wall isn’t built to carry it

A typical block boundary wall is designed to stand up, not to carry a tank. A storage wall should stand on its own footing, with any fixing to a wall or posts designed for the purpose.

Wind loading

Stable when empty, too

Full, a storage wall is heavy. Empty, a panel about 2.25 metres long and 1.75 metres high is light and catches the wind like a fence panel. Anchoring and bracing should be designed for an exposed site, especially if it stands freely as a screen.

Drainage and overflow

The rain still has to go somewhere

In a downpour the wall fills quickly. The overflow must go back to the existing downpipe and drain or to a suitable soakaway, never onto a neighbour’s garden, a path or a wall. Check that the gutter and downpipe are sound before connecting anything.

Frost

Taps, pipes and pumps in winter

Irish winters are mild, but exposed taps, hoses and pumps can freeze. Many tanks have a low outlet for draining down, and many downpipe filters have a winter setting. Ask the maker what the tank itself is rated for.

Planning

Height limits, and a grey area

Garden walls and fences around a house are generally exempted development up to 2 metres high, or 1.2 metres in front of the house, subject to conditions. Whether a storage tank counts as a wall or fence isn’t settled. If in doubt, a Section 5 declaration from your local authority gives a formal answer.

Shared boundaries

Talk to your neighbour first

On or against a shared boundary, agree it with your neighbour before anything is built, including access for the work and for maintenance. Where the boundary wall itself is shared, it isn’t yours alone to load or alter.

Confirm individually

Practical guidance. The answers depend on your garden and the product.

InstallationWho should install it

For a full-sized storage wall, plan on professional installation: a landscaper or groundworker for the footing, the maker’s installation guidance for the tank and its fixings, a structural engineer where the load, the ground or the wind exposure calls for one, and a Registered Electrical Contractor for any pump wiring. Ask the maker whether its warranty depends on professional installation.

Roof areaWill it fill?

The roof area that drains to the chosen downpipe, multiplied by the rainfall in millimetres, gives a rough idea of how many litres could be collected. A large wall on a small roof may rarely fill; a small one on a big roof will overflow often. Your installer can size it to the roof.

SafetyChildren and pets

Choose a fully closed tank with a secure lid or no open top. Even a small depth of water is a drowning risk for a young child.

UpkeepLooking after it

Clear the gutter and the downpipe filter regularly, especially in autumn, and leave room to reach the filter, the tap and any pump. A closed, dark tank helps keep algae down.

MoneyWhat it won’t do

Irish households don’t currently pay domestic water charges, so a storage wall shouldn’t be expected to reduce a water bill. Its value is in having water for the garden through dry spells, watering plants with rainwater, using less treated water outdoors, and a boundary that does a job.

Questions to ask

Start with your garden, not with the tank.

Whoever you talk to, these questions help you compare like with like.

  1. How much roof drains to the downpipe we’d use, and what size of storage suits it?
  2. What footing does the filled weight need on our ground, and who designs it?
  3. Does it stand on its own, or fix to a wall or posts? Is that fixing designed for wind when it’s empty?
  4. Where does the overflow go, and can our gutter, downpipe and drain take it?
  5. Does it need planning permission or a Section 5 declaration here? Is the boundary shared?
  6. How is it protected from frost, and how is it drained down?
  7. If we add a pump, what power does it need, and who wires it?
  8. What does the warranty cover, and does it depend on professional installation?
  9. Who supplies spare filters and parts in Ireland?
