### 1.5 ★ THE BUILDING REGULATIONS CLIFF AT 25 m² — the most important finding

**Building Regulations 1997 (S.I. 497/1997), Article 8 and the Third Schedule.**
Article 8 exempts from the Building Regulations only *"a building referred to in the
Third Schedule"*. Third Schedule **Class 2** covers a single-storey building ancillary
to a dwelling — expressly *"such as a summer house, poultry-house, aviary,
conservatory, coal shed, garden tool shed or bicycle shed"* — subject to:

1. detached from any other building;
2. **floor area not exceeding 25 square metres**;
3. height not exceeding 3 m, or 4 m with a pitched roof;
4. used exclusively for recreational or storage purposes or keeping plants, birds or
   animals for domestic purposes, and **not for any trade or business or for human
   habitation**.

**The planning exemption rose to 30 m² on 27 July 2026. The Building Regulations
exemption did not move. It is still 25 m².**

Therefore a garden room of, say, 28 m² is **exempted development for planning purposes
and simultaneously subject to the full Building Regulations** — structure, fire,
ventilation, conservation of fuel and energy, the whole Second Schedule. A homeowner
reading any Irish source today would conclude the opposite.

Two further edges in the same Class 2: the exemption also fails if the building is
**attached** to any other building, and if it is used **for a trade or business** — so
a 20 m² garden room run as a commercial premises is outside the Building Regulations
exemption on use alone, at any size.

### 1.6 ★ Commencement Notices — the garden room and the garden home differ

**Building Control Regulations 1997 (S.I. 496/1997), Article 7(2)(a).** Part II
(commencement notices) *"shall not apply to works … where (i) the works are … exempted
development for the purposes of the [planning Acts], and (ii) Part III [fire safety
certificates] … do not apply."*

- **Class 3 garden room, 25–30 m²:** on the face of Article 7(2)(a) **no Commencement
  Notice is required**, because the works are exempted development and Part III does
  not apply. The Building Regulations standards still apply (1.5 above). Compliance is
  the owner's own obligation, with no notice, no assigned certifier and no completion
  certificate to police it. That gap is worth telling a homeowner about.
- **Class 3A garden home:** paragraph 4 of Class 3A **expressly disapplies that
  escape** — building control legislation applies, by name. And a garden home is *the
  design and construction of a new dwelling*, which under the **Building Control
  (Amendment) Regulations 2014 (S.I. 9/2014)**, Articles 9(1)(b) and 9(2)(a), 20A and
  20F, triggers the full chain: Commencement Notice with **Additional Documentation**,
  **Notices of Assignment** for the builder and the Assigned Certifier, Certificates of
  Compliance (Design), the preliminary inspection plan, and a **Certificate of
  Compliance on Completion** registered before use.

**Verification of the existing live claim.** PlotNua's "Two notices, not one" section
states, of the garden home: *"A Commencement Notice with Additional Documentation,
naming a competent builder and an assigned certifier"* and *"a Certificate of
Compliance on Completion has to be registered … before anybody can move in."*
**Verified correct** against Class 3A paragraph 4 and S.I. 9/2014. No correction needed.
It is now primary-source backed rather than asserted.

Limit on that finding, stated honestly: S.I. 9/2014 does not amend Article 7 (I
enumerated its amendments: Articles 5(4), 9, 10, 20A(2), 20A(3), 21, 23). I have **not**
checked every Building Control amendment instrument between 1997 and 2026 for an
amendment to Article 7, and the Sixth Schedule notice form carries a broader phrase —
*"the creation of a building"* — than the operative Article 9(2). See §2.

### 1.7 ★ How the area is measured — the question the law does not answer

**S.I. 600/2001, Article 3** defines one measurement term:

> *"gross floor space" means the area ascertained by the **internal measurement** of the
> floor space on each floor of a building (**including internal walls and partitions**),
> disregarding any floor space provided for the parking of vehicles…*

**Class 3 does not use that term.** Class 3 paragraph 2 says *"the total area of such
structures"*. Class 3A paragraph 7 says *"the total area"*; Class 1 says *"the floor
area"*. Mechanically verified: the phrase "floor area" appears **26 times** in
S.I. 600/2001 and is **never defined**; no definition of "total area" exists either;
the Part 2 interpretation article (Article 5) does not supply one.

So the honest answer is: **the Regulations define a measurement term, and the
garden-structure classes do not use it.** That is why a council can say
external-including-overhang while retailers say internal, and both can point at the
text. The practical advice that follows is sound and does not require resolving the
ambiguity: measure both ways, assume the larger, and if you are within a couple of
square metres of 30 m² get a Section 5 declaration before you order.

This document does **not** claim the ambiguity proves any particular method.

### 1.8 Article 9 restrictions — what actually bites on a garden building

**S.I. 600/2001, Article 9(1)**, read in full. The sub-paragraphs that can realistically
defeat a Class 3 or Class 3A exemption:

- **9(1)(a)(i)** — contravening a condition attached to a permission, or being
  inconsistent with a use specified in a permission. **The most commonly triggered one**
  on modern estates.
- 9(1)(a)(ii) — forming or materially widening an access to a public road whose
  surfaced carriageway exceeds 4 m. (Class 3A paragraph 10 bars new access anyway.)
- 9(1)(a)(iii) — endangering public safety by traffic hazard.
- 9(1)(a)(iv) — bringing a building forward of the front wall / building line on a
  street. (Class 3 paragraph 1 covers this too.)
- 9(1)(a)(vi) — interfering with a landscape, view or prospect of special amenity value
  whose preservation is **an objective of the development plan**.
- 9(1)(a)(vii) — excavation or alteration of archaeological, geological, historical,
  scientific or ecological features whose preservation is **an objective of the
  development plan**.
- **9(1)(a)(viii)** — the extension, alteration, repair or renewal of an **unauthorised
  structure**, or a structure whose use is unauthorised. You cannot launder an
  unauthorised building through Class 3.
- 9(1)(a)(x) — fencing or enclosing land habitually open to the public for 10 years.
- 9(1)(a)(xi) — obstructing a public right of way.
- **9(1)(a)(xii)** — works to the **exterior of a structure** located in an
  Architectural Conservation Area where the development would materially affect the
  character of the area.
- **9(1)(b)** — in an area subject to a **Special Amenity Area Order**, development of
  "class 1, 3, 11, 16, 21, 22, 27, 28, 29, 31, 33(c), 39, 44 or 50(a)" is not exempt.
  **Class 3 is expressly listed. Class 3A is not** — the numbering was not updated when
  Class 3A was inserted. See §2.
- 9(1)(c) EIA, 9(1)(d) major accident hazards — not relevant to a domestic garden.

### 1.9 Protected structures and ACAs — and a correction to the live page

- **Section 2, PDA 2000**: *"protected structure" means (a) a structure, or (b) a
  specified part of a structure, which is included in a record of protected structures,
  **and, where that record so indicates, includes any specified feature which is within
  the attendant grounds** of the structure and which would not otherwise be included.*
- **Section 57(1)**: works to a protected structure or proposed protected structure are
  exempted development **only if** they would not materially affect the character of the
  structure or of any element contributing to its special interest.
- **Section 57(2)–(3)**: the owner or occupier may ask the planning authority in writing
  for a declaration as to what works would or would not materially affect the character;
  the authority **shall issue it within 12 weeks**. No fee is specified in s.57.
- **Section 82(1)**: works to the **exterior of a structure** located in an ACA are
  exempted development only if they would not materially affect the character of the
  area.

**CORRECTION REQUIRED — existing live content.** The page's "Protected structures and
conservation areas" drawer currently states: *"If your house is a protected structure,
the protection extends to the land in its curtilage and to other structures on that
land."* Section 2 makes that conditional: the attendant-grounds extension applies
**only where the Record of Protected Structures so indicates**, and only to features the
record specifies. As written, the sentence is an overstatement. Proposed replacement in
§4, change **C4**.

### 1.10 The "7 year rule" — verified, and it is not what people think

**Section 157(4)(a), PDA 2000**: *"No warning letter or enforcement notice shall issue
and no proceedings for an offence under this Part shall commence — (i) in respect of a
development where no permission has been granted, after seven years from the date of
the commencement of the development…"* Section 157(5): other offences, 7 years from
commission. Section 157(4)(b): proceedings may be commenced **at any time** in respect
of a condition concerning the **use of land**.

Mechanically confirmed: the phrase "seven year" does not appear in S.I. 600/2001 — the
rule is in the Act, not the Regulations.

What it is: a **bar on enforcement**. What it is not: authorisation. It does not make
unauthorised development lawful, does not produce any certificate, and does not satisfy
a purchaser's solicitor. It is not a route anyone should plan around, and the page
should say so in one sentence rather than ignore it — because homeowners are being told
about it on forums.

### 1.11 Section 5 declaration — the route that settles everything above

**Section 5, PDA 2000**, read in full:

- Any person may request a written declaration from the planning authority on whether
  something is or is not development, or is or is not exempted development, **on payment
  of the prescribed fee** (s.5(1)).
- The authority **shall** issue the declaration **and the main reasons and
  considerations** within **4 weeks** of the request (s.5(2)(a)); if it requires further
  information, within **3 weeks** of receiving it (s.5(2)(b)).
- The applicant may refer the declaration to An Bord Pleanála for review within **4
  weeks** (s.5(3)(a)); if no declaration issues, the question may be referred within 4
  weeks of the date it was due (s.5(3)(b)).
- Declarations are entered in the planning register (s.5(5)).

Note the contrast with s.57: a **Section 5** declaration carries a prescribed fee and a
4-week clock; a **Section 57** protected-structure declaration has no fee specified and
a 12-week clock. The page should not conflate them.

## 2 · REMAINING UNCERTAINTIES — to be published as unresolved, not guessed

1. **Does Class 3A apply to semi-detached and terraced houses?** The Class heading says
   *"the rear garden of a **principal house**"*, but paragraph 7 says *"within the rear
   garden of the curtilage of a **detached house**"*. The two are inconsistent on the
   face of the text. Thousands of homeowners will ask this. **Unresolved.**
2. **Internal or external measurement** for "total area" / "floor area". Undefined.
   §1.7. **Unresolved, and the reason is in the drafting.**
3. **Does a toilet breach Class 3's incidental-use test?** Class 3 says nothing about
   sanitary fittings. The operative constraint is the use test in paragraph 6. Whether a
   WC alone is inconsistent with "incidental to the enjoyment of the house as such" is
   an interpretation question that primary law does not answer. **Unresolved.** (For
   Class 3A the position *is* answered: paragraph 12 permits water and wastewater but
   bars a **separate connection**.)
4. **Is a Commencement Notice required for a 25–30 m² Class 3 garden room?** Article
   7(2)(a) says no. But the Sixth Schedule notice form's accompanying note uses the
   broader phrase *"the creation of a building (including a single dwelling unit)"*,
   which on its face would catch a new garden building. Operative Article 9(2) limits
   Additional Documentation to a new dwelling, a >40 m² extension, or Part III works.
   Councils may read this differently. **Unresolved in practice; the text points one
   way.**
5. **Does a Class 3A garden home need a Fire Safety Certificate?** Class 3A paragraph 4
   says Building Regulations *"including Fire Safety"* apply — that is **Part B of the
   Second Schedule**, which is a standard. A Fire Safety Certificate is a separate thing
   under **Part III of the Building Control Regulations**, which does not generally
   apply to a dwelling house. Not verified to primary source for this new class.
   **Unresolved.**
6. **Is Class 3 barred but Class 3A permitted in a Special Amenity Area Order area?**
   Article 9(1)(b)(i) lists class numbers and was not updated when Class 3A was
   inserted. §1.8. **Unresolved — looks like a drafting omission; we will not assert
   either reading.**
7. **Does Article 9(1)(a)(xii) / s.82 catch a new detached garden building in an ACA?**
   Both are worded as works to the **exterior of a structure**. A standalone new
   building is arguably not that. **Unresolved — a Section 5 declaration settles it.**
8. **Has Article 7 of the Building Control Regulations 1997 been amended since 1997?**
   Verified not amended by S.I. 9/2014. Not checked against every intervening Building
   Control instrument. **Partially verified.**
9. **Planning and Development Act 2024 commencement.** The s.5 and s.157 routes above
   are from the 2000 Act as enacted. The 2024 Act restates parts of this architecture.
   Which provisions are commenced today, and whether the section numbers a homeowner
   will be quoted have changed, is **not verified**. The page should cite the
   substance, not the section number, wherever it can.

**None of these will be guessed on the page. Each gets the words "the regulations do
not say" or equivalent, which is both true and the most useful sentence available.**
